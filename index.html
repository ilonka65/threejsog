It’s All One Place

It’s All One Place explores the 1% remnant of a Gondwana rainforest that once spanned 75,000 hectares of Northern NSW known as “The Big Scrub”. This environmental work offers a public-facing portal into complex scientific material usually locked inside specialist formats that together, hold the history and ecological reality of the Big Scrub rainforest.

The App

The Big Scrub boundary is topographically mapped to display the 79 surviving remnants, five of which are reconstructed into three-dimensional canopy surfaces via LiDAR point clouds. Data includes groundbreaking genetic rescue science for improving rainforest restoration by preserving the tree lineages of the ancient Gondwanan world. Field recordings animate the Indo-Malesian and Gondwana genomic streams, which activate when hovered over, revealing a UI card displaying each tree’s scientific name, common name, lineage ancestry, and conservation attributes.

The app uses custom Three.js (WebGL) creative code to dynamically stream raw scientific data. The interactive environment synthesises multiple layers of data including:

  • The Original Boundary and 79 Remnants: The visual foundation tracks the historical 75,000-hectare boundary of the ancient, pre-clearing Big Scrub forest. Plotted within this footprint are all 79 surviving remnants.
  • The Genomic Data: Using chloroplast DNA sequences, the project generates 3D particle streams in visually distinct colour nodes separating ancient Gondwanan plant lineages from migratory Indo-Malesian species.
  • 3D LiDAR Point Clouds: Incorporating high-density 3D LiDAR scans for five primary remnants, the environment renders each point cloud as an interactive field of particles.

Future Iterations

Future developments of this project will expand the digital architecture to include full 3D LiDAR reconstructions of all 79 remaining fragments. It will also allow viewers to locate these rainforest remnants across modern subdivided land within the historical Big Scrub boundary, utilising:

  • Standardised LiDAR Resolution: Homogenising the point-cloud density across all reserves (currently constrained by edge-network hosting limits) to ensure a uniform holographic aesthetic.
  • Location-specific botanical survey data integration: Reflect the actual composition of the Gondwana Stream (Vibrant Green) and The Indo-Malesian Stream (Luminous Blue) for each reserve via the UI cards and 3D Point Cloud shaders, which are currently averaged across the work based on the overall percentage of each stream.
  • Street & Suburban Basemaps: Providing modern navigational layers and familiar landmarks to physically ground the historical ecology in the present day.
  • Historical Property Data: Overlaying modern regional cadastral subdivisions to contrast against historical 1910 land parcels and historical parish maps, revealing colonial agricultural subdivision lines.
  • Google Satellite: True-colour environmental basemaps.
  • An Interactive Panel: Operating through Makey Makey physical touchpoints, an interactive control surface and contextual guide, visualised on a second projection.
